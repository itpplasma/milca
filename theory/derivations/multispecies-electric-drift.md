# Incompressible electric drift with conserving collisions

The energy-coupled surface solver needs one physical electric parameter for
all speed nodes and species. A common monoenergetic parameter would assign
different physical electric fields to different speeds.

Let \(B=B_0b(\theta,\zeta)\), with Boozer angles \(\theta,\zeta\),
rotational transform \(\iota=1/q\), covariant field components \(G,I\),
electrostatic potential \(\Phi(\psi_t)\) and toroidal flux \(2\pi\psi_t\).
The flux-surface average has weight \(b^{-2}\). Define

\[
\Omega_E=\Phi'G/(G+\iota I),\qquad
E=\frac{b^2}{\langle b^2\rangle}
  [\partial_\theta-(I/G)\partial_\zeta]. \tag{1}
\]

Here \(\Phi'=d\Phi/d\psi_t\), and \(\Omega_E\) has units of inverse
seconds. Species \(a\) has mass \(m_a\), temperature \(T_a\) in energy
units, density \(n_a\), charge \(Z_ae\), thermal speed
\(v_{ta}=\sqrt{2T_a/m_a}\), normalized speed
\(x=v/v_{ta}\), and nodal weight \(w_k\). Let \(C_l\) be the existing
row-scaled collision matrix for Legendre degree \(l\), including species
and speed coupling. Let \(S\) denote streaming and mirror transport per
unit particle speed. The distribution response is
\(f_{1a}=F_a h_a\), where
\(F_a=n_a\exp(-x^2)/(\pi^{3/2}v_{ta}^3)\),
\(h_a=\sum_l h_{al}P_l(\xi)\), \(\xi=v_\parallel/v\), and
\(P_l\) is the Legendre polynomial of degree \(l\).

## Row scaling

The lumped speed moments and row factor used by the surface solver are

\[
m_{p,k}=w_kx_k^p e^{-x_k^2},\qquad
\rho_{ka}=1/(v_{ta}M_{3,kk}). \tag{2}
\]

Here \(M_3\) is the consistent Galerkin speed-mass matrix. Streaming is
lumped with \(m_3\), while electric transport is lumped with \(m_2\).
The streaming coefficient and electric coefficient of a row are therefore

\[
R_{ka}=\rho_{ka}v_{ta}m_{3,k}. \tag{3}
\]
\[
W_{ka}=\rho_{ka}\Omega_E m_{2,k}
=R_{ka}\frac{\Omega_E}{v_{ta}x_k}. \tag{4}
\]

The resulting operator is

\[
Ah=S hR+E hW-C h, \tag{5}
\]

where \(R,W\) are diagonal in the flattened speed-species index. The
notation in (5) treats surface-pitch rows as the first index and the
speed-species index as the second. Collision multiplication includes the
appropriate transpose when that matrix convention is used in code.
The physical collision operator and its conservation correction are unchanged.
The coefficient in (4), its forward derivative and its reverse derivative
are generated by `gen/gen_dk_mono.f90`.

Let \(\nu_{Da}(v)\) be the Lorentz deflection rate in
\(C_L=(\nu_{Da}/2)\partial_\xi[(1-\xi^2)\partial_\xi]\).
For Lorentz scattering, speed nodes decouple and (5) reduces to the
monoenergetic equation at

\[
\nu_{ka}=\nu_{Da}(v_{ta}x_k)/(v_{ta}x_k),\qquad
\omega_{E,ka}=\Omega_E/(v_{ta}x_k). \tag{6}
\]

Equation (6) supplies the independent velocity-integral reference. A fixed
\(\omega_E\) across the nodes does not solve the same electric-field problem.

## Density constraints and implicit derivative

The existing density-source border uses one multiplier \(\mu_{ka}\) and
one constraint \(\langle h_{0,ka}\rangle=0\) per speed-species node. The
electric term is skew in the surface measure and annihilates constants,
so it adds no particle source to these averaged equations.
Source multipliers remain discrete solvability diagnostics.

At fixed magnetic field, species, speed grid, forces and collision matrix, write
\(h_E=\partial h/\partial\Omega_E\) and
\(W_E=\partial W/\partial\Omega_E\). Let
\(\mu_E=\partial\mu/\partial\Omega_E\) be the source derivative.
The bordered tangent solves

\[
Ah_E+\mu_E e_0=-E hW_E,\qquad
\langle(h_E)_{0,ka}\rangle=0. \tag{7}
\]

The vector \(e_0\) inserts each node's constant degree-zero source.
The same factors and Krylov preconditioner are reused. The transport moments
contain no explicit \(\Omega_E\), so their derivatives are the same moments
of \(h_E\).

## Modal preconditioner

Let \(V\) diagonalize \(R^{-1}C_4\), as in the zero-field solver. The
exact transformed electric matrix is

\[
\widetilde W=V^{-1}R^{-1}WV. \tag{8}
\]

Its diagonal is retained in each monoenergetic preconditioning factor.
Off-diagonal entries are omitted only from the preconditioner. The Krylov
operator retains the full nodal electric action, so this approximation does
not alter the solved physics. For Lorentz scattering each node is independent.
Its preconditioner is exact when the bordered monoenergetic factorization
inverts the corresponding node block to roundoff.

## Transport normalization and reciprocity

Let \(b_i\) be the field on angular node \(i\), \(J_0=\sum_i b_i^{-2}\),
\(\tau_a=T_a/(Z_ae)\), and
\(s_i=[\partial_\theta b-(I/G)\partial_\zeta b]_i/q\).
For a response to one unit drive, define the pitch moments

\[
\mathcal F(h)=\sum_i\frac{2s_i}{b_i^3}
 (8h_{0i}/3+4h_{2i}/15),\qquad
\mathcal Q(h)=\sum_i\frac{4h_{1i}}{3b_i}. \tag{9}
\]

The returned matrix \(D_{ab,ij}\) maps drive \(j\) of species \(b\)
to moment \(i\) of species \(a\). Drives 1 and 2 have radial angular
factor \((1+\xi^2)s/b\) with speed weights \(\tau_b m_4\) and \(\tau_b m_6\).
Drive 3 is the parallel-electric angular factor \(\xi b\) with weight
\(v_{tb}m_3\). The code applies its common row scaling to each drive.
The three returned moments are

\[
D_{ab,1j}=\frac{\tau_a}{2\sqrt\pi J_0}
 \sum_k m_{4,k}\mathcal F(h^{bj}_{ak}),\qquad
D_{ab,2j}=\frac{\tau_a}{2\sqrt\pi J_0}
 \sum_k m_{6,k}\mathcal F(h^{bj}_{ak}), \tag{10}
\]
\[
D_{ab,3j}=\frac{v_{ta}}{2\sqrt\pi J_0}
 \sum_k m_{3,k}\mathcal Q(h^{bj}_{ak}). \tag{11}
\]

Here \(h^{bj}\) is the distribution response to that drive. At equal
background temperatures the Onsager-Casimir relation is

\[
n_aD_{ab,ij}(\Omega_E)=p_i p_j n_bD_{ba,ji}(-\Omega_E),\qquad
(p_1,p_2,p_3)=(1,1,-1). \tag{12}
\]

## Verification and limits

| Check | Observed error | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Lorentz transport against independent monoenergetic speed integration at zero and both field signs | below \(10^{-13}\) relative | \(10^{-9}\) |
| Equation (7) against centered differences of nine full-collision transport entries | \(2.8\times10^{-5}\) relative with a small-entry floor | \(2\times10^{-4}\) |
| Equal-temperature deuterium-electron and helium-electron Onsager-Casimir relation, reversed field, \(17\times11\) angles | \(4.6\times10^{-5}\) and \(3.5\times10^{-7}\) relative | \(2\times10^{-4}\) |

The \(11\times7\) angular grid fails the last criterion at
\(1.9\times10^{-3}\). The finer result is a resolved test of this matrix
symmetry, not a general convergence bound for the transport coefficients.
Existing zero-field surface checks remain passing.

The Lorentz and reciprocity errors divide each entry's absolute difference
by the absolute reference entry. The tangent error uses the larger of the
centered derivative magnitude and \(10^{-4}|D|/|\Omega_E|\).
All Lorentz comparisons use \(\Omega_E=0,\pm4000\) inverse seconds.
The tangent and reciprocity checks use \(4000\) inverse seconds.
The deuterium test has equal ion and electron densities. The doubly charged
helium test has twice as many electrons as ions and uses the density weights
in (12).

`test_ntv_surface electric trace.csv` writes the radial coefficient from
the energy-coupled solve and the independent monoenergetic speed integral.
Its unit is volt squared seconds, equivalent to toroidal-flux squared per
second when toroidal flux is divided by \(2\pi\).

This extension retains the incompressible electric approximation of
[Landreman et al. (2014)](https://arxiv.org/html/1312.6058v2), equations
(16), (18) and (20). It does not add electric speed or pitch transport,
tangential magnetic drift, radial orbit coupling, physical loss boundaries
or a self-consistent electric field. Momentum conservation of the collision
matrix does not establish intrinsic ambipolarity for arbitrary reduced
trajectories at finite electric field.
