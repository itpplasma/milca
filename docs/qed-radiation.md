## Why

Charged particles exchange energy and momentum through emitted and absorbed
photons as well as through the Coulomb field. This exchange can be closed
locally, like Coulomb collisions, whose fields are screened within a Debye
length, only where photons are absorbed close to where they are emitted, as at
the fundamental cyclotron harmonic in a hot dense plasma. Where the photon mean
free path is comparable to the gradient lengths or the device size, as for
higher harmonics, synchrotron emission and bremsstrahlung, the exchange couples
velocity space to the radiation field over large distances.

Three applications need it:

- Fusion fuels beyond D-T. D-³He and p-¹¹B burn only at ion temperatures of
  tens to hundreds of keV, where relativistic electron-ion and electron-electron
  bremsstrahlung and synchrotron emission decide whether fusion power exceeds
  the losses (Putvinski, Ryutov and Yushmanov 2019).
- Relativistic electrons in tokamaks: radiation reaction and bremsstrahlung
  limit runaway energies and change the critical field.
- Astrophysical plasmas near compact objects, where electron temperatures reach
  the electron rest energy and electron-positron pairs are created and
  annihilated (Svensson 1982).

## Hierarchy

The levels form a graph rather than a ladder: each names its parent model,
and its defect, the measured difference of an observable from the parent's, is
reported as in the [collision hierarchy](collisions.md). An oracle is an
independent reference result such as a closed form or a parent model.

**Q0. QED in a box.** Electron, positron and photon modes in a finite periodic
volume in Coulomb gauge, with nuclei as heavy charged particles or static
Coulomb sources, evolved in real time in Fock space, extending the
[finite-basis fermion](quantum-fock.md) branch. Mode truncation, the momentum
cutoff and mass and charge renormalization are explicit; showing that their
errors can be controlled, and that tree-level and one-loop results are
recovered, is part of building this level. It is meant as the microscopic
reference for small systems: scattering, radiation reaction, and pair creation
in strong fields.

**Q1. Perturbative QED cross sections.** Tree-level amplitudes and
differential cross sections generated by FortSym, MILCA's symbolic code
generator: Møller and Bhabha
scattering, Compton scattering (Klein-Nishina), electron-nucleus
bremsstrahlung with screening (Bethe and Heitler 1934), electron-electron
bremsstrahlung (Haug 1975), photon-photon pair creation (Breit and Wheeler
1934) and pair annihilation; then one-loop radiative corrections and the
soft-photon treatment (Berestetskii, Lifshitz and Pitaevskii). Each is checked
against its closed form and its limits: Thomson scattering, Rutherford and
Mott scattering, and the nonrelativistic Born limit of bremsstrahlung (the
Kramers formula times the Born Gaunt factor).

**Q2. QED kinetics.** Coupled Boltzmann equations for electrons, positrons,
photons and ions with the Q1 kernels, including Pauli blocking and induced
emission. The relativistic Landau and Beliaev-Budker operators of the
[collision hierarchy](collisions.md) are its small-angle limit (Braams and
Karney 1987), which makes the existing operators oracles for Q2 and Q2 an
oracle for them.

**Q3. Classical radiation reaction.** The Landau-Lifshitz force (Landau and
Lifshitz, *Classical Theory of Fields*, 4th English edition, §76) in the
particle push of the [Maxwell-Lorentz many-body reference](microscopic-many-body.md),
declared as the physical model of the singular point-charge self-force rather
than hidden in numerical softening. Its parent is photon emission in a
background field (strong-field QED below), in the limit of a small quantum
parameter χ, the ratio of the field in the particle's rest frame to the
Schwinger critical field.

**Q4. Radiation collision operator.** The Fokker-Planck operator for electrons
exchanging cyclotron-harmonic photons in a magnetized plasma, with its
friction, diffusion and integral parts. The friction-only reduction (the
synchrotron reaction force) is a declared model whose defect is measured
against the full operator; the ratio of radiative to Coulomb pitch-angle
scattering is the ordering parameter to compute. Because the photon mean free
path exceeds the Larmor radius, the operator carries the photon distribution and
couples to radiation transport instead of closing locally.

**Q5. Photon transport.** Reduced from Q2 and Q4 for the photons: the R0-R4 ladder of [radiation](radiation.md) for
bremsstrahlung, synchrotron emission with wall reflection (Trubnikov 1979) and
Compton scattering. The emission side runs: cyclotron and synchrotron emission
harmonic by harmonic (Schott) and the thermal emissivity j(ω, θ) of a Juttner
plasma (`milca_cyclotron_emission`), both reproducing the Larmor power; the
absorption by Kirchhoff's law and the transport with reflecting walls follow.

**Q6. Losses in fluid and transport models.** Emissivities and loss rates for
relativistic Maxwellian and non-Maxwellian electrons, reduced from Q2, Q4 and
Q5; the radiation sinks of the existing transport and discharge models become
instances with stated validity.

**Strong-field QED.** A separate branch from Q0: the strong background field
is included exactly (Furry picture, Volkov states) instead of perturbatively,
which gives quantum synchrotron emission and pair creation in strong magnetic
fields (Erber 1966) for χ of order one, and the Monte Carlo emission rates of
QED particle-in-cell codes. It is the parent of Q3. The spin-averaged
emission rate in the locally constant field approximation runs
(`milca_strong_field`); its power reduces to the Landau-Lifshitz value with
the Gaunt factor 1 - (55√3/16)χ + 48χ², and so does pair creation by a
photon, whose rate reaches (3√3/(16√2))χ e^{-8/(3χ)} at small χ.

## First slice (P1g)

Question: the relaxation and power balance of a hot magnetized
electron-ion plasma with radiation, at p-¹¹B and D-³He conditions and at
runaway energies.

1. Q1 kernels for electron-ion and electron-electron bremsstrahlung, Møller,
   Compton, pair creation and annihilation from generated amplitudes, checked
   against their published closed forms and limits.
2. Bremsstrahlung loss rates of relativistic Maxwellian electrons (electron-ion
   and electron-electron) by direct integration of the Q1 cross sections,
   checked against published fits.
3. Radiation friction in the relativistic collision operator, then the full Q4
   operator at the fundamental and higher harmonics with a declared photon
   distribution (optically thin, or a prescribed one) until Q5 couples it; the
   defect of the friction-only reduction against the Coulomb scattering it
   competes with.
4. Thermal pair balance as a function of temperature, density and optical
   depth, compared with Svensson (1982). The optically thick end runs:
   Fermi-Dirac pairs in chemical equilibrium with photons at fixed net charge
   (`pair_equilibrium`), and pair creation in a Wien photon gas
   (`photon_pair_rate`), which balances thermal annihilation by detailed
   balance between the Breit-Wheeler and Dirac cross sections. The optically
   thin end, where pairs come from e-e, e-p and photon-particle collisions
   and the photons escape, follows.
5. The p-¹¹B and D-³He power balance: fusion power from published
   reactivities (Bosch and Hale 1992; Sikora and Weller 2016; Putvinski et al.
   2019) and, once available, from [nuclear reactions](nuclear-reactions.md),
   against bremsstrahlung and synchrotron losses, with wall reflection through
   a declared reflection coefficient (Trubnikov 1979) until Q5 is in place.

Gate: the kernels of step 1 agree with their closed forms to rounding; the loss
rates of step 2 agree with the published fits within the accuracy those fits
state; every later step reports its defect against its parent.

## First result: p-¹¹B at equal temperatures

`app/pb11_power_balance` (sCluster, 2026-11-15) compares the p-¹¹B fusion
power with electron-ion and electron-electron bremsstrahlung at equal
electron and ion temperature, boron fractions x = n_B/n_p of 0.1 to 0.3,
electron-ion bremsstrahlung with the Coulomb correction of Elwert:

| T (keV) | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 | 300 | 500 | 800 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| best P_fus/P_brems | 0.032 | 0.21 | 0.37 | 0.42 | 0.38 | 0.26 | 0.17 |
| optimal x | 0.094 | 0.101 | 0.106 | 0.110 | 0.115 | 0.119 | 0.118 |
| P_ee/P_ei | 0.09 | 0.18 | 0.26 | 0.34 | 0.44 | 0.51 | 0.49 |

Bremsstrahlung alone exceeds the fusion power at every temperature, as
Putvinski, Ryutov and Yushmanov (2019) found. The boron fraction enters only
through the densities, n_p = n_e/(1 + 5x) and n_B = x n_p, so the optimum
follows from the computed rates: it is x = 1/√125 ≈ 0.089 when only
electron-ion bremsstrahlung counts and moves to 0.10-0.12 with the
electron-electron part. The Coulomb correction raises electron-ion
bremsstrahlung by 5.6 % at 50 keV and 1.7 % at 200 keV against Born. Not yet
included: synchrotron losses and electron temperatures below the ion
temperature.
